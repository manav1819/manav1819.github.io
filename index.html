About Me

I hold a Bachelor of Applied Information Sciences in Information Systems Security from Sheridan College, where I led a four-person capstone team through the full delivery of a security-awareness platform — from architecture to production deployment. Alongside my studies, I've worked full-time in physical security at a data center site, which gave me a practical, ground-level understanding of access control, monitoring, and incident response long before I applied it in a SOC context.

My work spans both sides of the fence: defensive practices like detection engineering and threat modeling, and offensive fundamentals like exploit development and penetration testing, because understanding how systems are broken is what makes defending them meaningful. Cybersecurity is an ever-evolving field — new vulnerabilities, new frameworks, new attack surfaces — and I treat continuous learning as a core part of the job, not a phase that ends at graduation.

My approach is rooted in three principles: clarity in communication, empathy for the people security exists to protect, and unwavering integrity in every engagement.

Completed

Group Capstone Research

Semester-long research project analyzing 2026 MCP STDIO supply-chain RCE vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-30615, CVE-2026-30623): CVSS v4.0 scoring, detection engineering mapped to MITRE ATT&CK/ATLAS, an original Sigma detection rule, STRIDE threat modeling across six trust boundaries in OWASP Threat Dragon, and an original Agent Trust Boundary Compromise Framework (ATBCF).

TraceLite

Personal / Open Source

An open-source Bitcoin fund-flow tracer, built after losing funds to a scam and wanting the tooling to exist. Deliberately non-attributional — it tracks where funds moved, not who owns the wallets. v1 covers UTXO-model traversal via free block-explorer APIs, depth-limited hops, fan-in/fan-out heuristics, graph visualization, and JSON/PDF reporting.
